# Global semiconductor investing for an India-resident investor

**Research date:** 25 August 2026  
**Investor context assumed:** resident and ordinarily resident (ROR) individual in India, non-US person, long-term investor, cash accounts at Interactive Brokers (IBKR) and Paasa.  
**Purpose:** decision support, not a personal recommendation or tax/legal advice. Fund holdings, prices, AUM and tax rules change; verify the ISIN and order preview before trading and confirm tax treatment with an India/US cross-border professional.

## Executive conclusion

For a long holding period, the cleanest default instrument is usually an **Ireland-domiciled, accumulating UCITS semiconductor ETF**, bought on a liquid European listing through IBKR or Paasa. The strongest broad candidate found is **iShares MSCI Global Semiconductors UCITS ETF (ISIN IE000I8KRLL9)**: it owns more than 300 securities, includes Taiwan and Korea directly, accumulates distributions, costs 0.35%, and had about USD 5.7bn of assets in August 2026. The concentrated alternative is **VanEck Semiconductor UCITS ETF (IE00BMC38736)**: 25 US-listed pure plays, 0.35%, roughly USD 8.3bn, but its top ten were about 78% of assets in April and it omits much of the long tail.

US ETFs are easier to trade and include excellent products, but an Indian non-US investor should not choose one without explicitly accepting **US estate-tax situs** and dividend-withholding friction. If that risk is accepted, **SOXQ** is the low-fee concentrated option (0.19%), **SOXX** is the established 30-name benchmark (0.33%), **XSD** is the more equal-weight/smaller-company expression (0.35%), and **SMH** is the most mega-cap/Nvidia-heavy expression (0.35%).

The “selloff” premise needs nuance. On 21 August SOXX was at USD 520 versus a 52-week NAV high of USD 655.22 (about **20.6% below the high**), yet its YTD NAV return was **+73.7%** and its portfolio traded at **67.8x P/E** on 14 August. The global UCITS iShares fund was about **18.5% below** its 52-week high on 7 August, but was still **+77.3% YTD**. This looks like a correction from a volatile, strongly appreciating and still richly valued sector—not evidence by itself of a broad bargain. ## Direct-stock map across the value chain

Direct stocks are appropriate only if the investor has a differentiated view on a bottleneck or company. A basket should span layers; buying Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom alone is a fabless/AI-compute bet, not a balanced semiconductor thesis.

| Layer | Representative investable companies / primary listing | What drives the thesis | Principal risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| EDA and IP | Synopsys (SNPS, US), Cadence (CDNS, US), Arm (ARM ADR, US) | Toll-road economics on chip complexity | High multiples, customer concentration/export controls |
| Fabless compute/connectivity | Nvidia (NVDA), AMD (AMD), Broadcom (AVGO), Qualcomm (QCOM), MediaTek (2454 Taiwan) | AI accelerators, networking, edge/mobile | Architecture shifts, hyperscaler insourcing, cyclicality |
| Memory | Micron (MU), SK Hynix (000660 Korea), Samsung Electronics (005930 Korea), Kioxia (285A Japan) | HBM and memory-cycle recovery | Severe commodity cycles and capex overshoot |
| Foundry / IDM | TSMC (2330 Taiwan / TSM ADR), Samsung, Intel (INTC), Texas Instruments (TXN), Infineon (IFX Germany), STMicroelectronics (STM US ADR / STMPA Paris), NXP (NXPI) | Leading-node scarcity; auto/industrial electrification | Taiwan geopolitics, huge capex, mature-node inventory cycles |
| Lithography / wafer-fab equipment | ASML (ASML Amsterdam/ADR), Applied Materials (AMAT), Lam Research (LRCX), KLA (KLAC), Tokyo Electron (8035 Japan), Advantest (6857 Japan) | Equipment intensity per advanced wafer; packaging/test | Export controls and synchronized fab-capex downturn |
| Materials / wafers | Shin-Etsu Chemical (4063 Japan), SUMCO (3436 Japan), Entegris (ENTG), GlobalWafers (6488 Taiwan) | Consumables and constrained specialty inputs | Pricing normalization, customer concentration |
| Packaging / test / OSAT | ASE Technology (3711 Taiwan / ASX ADR), Amkor (AMKR), BE Semiconductor (BESI Amsterdam), Teradyne (TER) | Advanced packaging and chiplets | Capacity cycles and customer bargaining power |

Before choosing a direct stock, compare: revenue exposure to AI versus legacy end-markets; gross margin through the last down-cycle; free cash flow after capex; net cash/debt; customer and geography concentration; export-control exposure; and forward earnings based on normalized—not peak-cycle—margins. Live company-level valuation multiples were not reproducibly available from primary issuer/exchange pages in this research pass, so none are invented here. Use IBKR's live fundamentals plus the latest company filings and reconcile denominator dates before acting.

## India-resident access, tax and estate considerations

### Broker and market access

- IBKR India says Indian residents trading overseas use a **cash account**, can trade stocks, bonds and ETFs in more than 90 markets, and global products are provided by its affiliate Interactive Brokers LLC. Availability is contract- and residency-specific; search by **ISIN**, not just ticker, because UCITS tickers repeat. [IBKR India individual accounts](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.in/en/accounts/individual-account-india.php) [IBKR contract search](https://www.interactivebrokers.co.in/en/trading/products-exchanges.php)
- Paasa states that holdings and cash are held at IBKR in the customer's name/PAN. Its published direct-trading exchanges include US, UK, Switzerland, Hong Kong, Germany, France, Netherlands, Canada, Japan and Singapore; it supports global stocks/ETFs and Ireland-domiciled UCITS listings, but not crypto or leveraged ETFs. [Paasa platform](https://paasa.com/) [Paasa help centre](https://paasa.com/help-center)
- Because Paasa is built on IBKR custody, two accounts do not create independent custodian diversification. Compare total FX spread, commission/minimum, market-data cost, tax-report convenience and exact contract permissions.

### LRS and funding

RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme permits resident individuals to remit up to **USD 250,000 per financial year** for permitted current or capital-account transactions, including a combination of both. Broker funding, other foreign investments, travel, education and other LRS uses share the limit. Use an authorised-dealer bank and the correct purpose code; do not assume margin funding is permitted. [RBI LRS notification](https://systemhealth.rbi.org.in/Scripts/NotificationUser.aspx_Id%3D9756%26Mode%3D0%281%29.html)

TCS on remittance is generally a **tax collection/credit, not the final tax cost**, but thresholds and rates have changed repeatedly. Confirm the bank's live LRS/TCS treatment for the remittance date and claim eligible credit in the ITR; this report intentionally does not state an unverified 2026 threshold.

### Indian income tax and reporting

- For foreign shares/ETF units that are not listed on a *recognised stock exchange in India*, the relevant long-term holding period is generally **more than 24 months**; shorter holdings are generally taxed at slab rates. The post-23 July 2024 long-term capital-gain rate is generally **12.5% without indexation**, subject to the precise asset and taxpayer facts, plus applicable surcharge/cess. The Income Tax Department's capital-gain materials describe the uniform 12.5% framework and 24-month treatment for other assets/unlisted shares. [CBDT capital-gains FAQ](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/documents/20117/14614766/FAQs%2B-New-Capital-Gains-Taxation-regime.pdf/ad59d362-7bce-f0aa-e483-ce0f61ac13d6?t=1767816817352) [Income Tax Department overview](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/en/sale-of-shares)
- Foreign dividends are generally included in Indian taxable income at the investor's applicable rate. Foreign tax withheld may be creditable subject to treaty, Form 67 and documentation. Accumulating UCITS funds remove cash distributions at investor level but do not automatically eliminate Indian tax questions.
- An ROR must report foreign custodian/depository accounts, equity/debt interests and foreign-source income in **Schedule FA/FSI**, and foreign-tax relief in **Schedule TR**. Schedule FA uses calendar-year information inside the Indian tax return, an easy mismatch to miss. [Income Tax Department Schedule FA guide](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/sites/default/files/2024-11/Enhancing%20Tax%20Transparency%20on%20Foreign%20Assets%20and%20Income.pdf) [ITR-2 manual](https://www.incometax.gov.in/iec/foportal/help/all-topics/e-filing-services/itr-2/itr-2-UM)

### US withholding and estate tax

- US-source dividends are ordinarily subject to 30% nonresident withholding unless treaty relief is properly claimed with **Form W-8BEN**. The US–India income-tax treaty should be checked for the applicable rate and ownership facts; ensure W-8BEN is current in each broker. [IRS treaty-claim process](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/claiming-tax-treaty-benefits) [IRS US–India treaty documents](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/international-businesses/india-tax-treaty-documents)
- For a nonresident non-US citizen, stock of US-organised corporations is US-situs even if held abroad or through a nominee. An executor generally must file Form 706-NA when US-situs assets exceed **USD 60,000**; India is not on the IRS list of estate-tax-treaty countries. This affects US companies and US-domiciled ETFs such as SOXX/SMH/XSD/SOXQ, not merely where the broker is located. [IRS estate-tax guidance](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/some-nonresidents-with-us-assets-must-file-estate-tax-returns) [IRS treaty-country list and situs explanation](https://www.irs.gov/statistics/soi-tax-stats-nonresident-alien-estate-tax-study-metadata)
- The USD 60,000 figure is a **filing threshold, not a promise that only the amount above it is taxed**, and the maximum estate-tax rate can reach 40%. Estate planning needs professional advice. An Irish UCITS ETF holding US shares generally changes the investor's direct asset from US stock to an Irish fund share, which is the central estate-planning attraction.

## Practical implementation framework

1. **Set a maximum thematic allocation first.** Semiconductors are cyclical, geopolitically concentrated and already a large implicit weight in global/US equity indexes. Count existing Nvidia/TSMC/tech-fund exposure before adding more.
2. **Pick domicile before ticker.** If US-situs assets may approach USD 60,000, make estate-tax treatment an explicit design constraint rather than an afterthought.
3. **Choose breadth deliberately.** A sensible core is the broad iShares global UCITS; a concentrated fund or 2–4 direct stocks is a satellite, not a duplicate core.
4. **Stage entry by rule, not narrative.** For example, divide the intended allocation into fixed tranches over 6–12 months or rebalance at preset portfolio weights. This reduces the need to identify the exact bottom.
5. **Use limit orders during the listing's local liquid hours.** Prefer the venue/currency with the tightest spread and meaningful turnover; trading currency is an operational choice, not a hedge of underlying exposure.
6. **Review quarterly:** valuation, top-ten weight, index methodology changes, country exposure, revenue/earnings revisions, and whether the original thesis—not the price alone—has changed.

## Follow-up: capture ratios and current fundamentals

### Method and data

The following is an independent, reproducible **market-price total-return** calculation, not an issuer-published statistic.

- **Benchmark:** iShares MSCI ACWI ETF (**ACWI**), used as a liquid USD proxy for MSCI ACWI. It is not the semiconductor funds' official benchmark.
- **Fund series:** split- and distribution-adjusted month-end market prices from Yahoo Finance's public chart endpoint, downloaded 25 August 2026. USD lines were SOXX, SMH, XSD, SOXQ, **SEMI.AS** (the USD Euronext Amsterdam listing of IE000I8KRLL9), and **SMH.L** (the USD London listing of IE00BMC38736). For 3119.HK, both the fund and ACWI were expressed in HKD using monthly HKD-per-USD FX.
- **Windows:** 36 complete monthly returns, **August 2023–July 2026**, and 60 complete monthly returns, **August 2021–July 2026**. The incomplete August 2026 observation was excluded. There were 25 ACWI-positive and 11 ACWI-negative months in the 3-year window; 38 positive and 22 negative months in the 5-year window.
- **Formula:** for the subset of benchmark-positive months, annualise the geometric linked fund return and divide it by the similarly annualised benchmark return: `up capture = [Π(1+Fund return)^(12/n)−1] / [Π(1+ACWI return)^(12/n)−1] × 100`. Down capture uses benchmark-negative months identically. A down capture above 100 means the fund lost more; a negative down capture means the fund produced a positive compound return across months when ACWI was down.
- **Important limitations:** exchange closes are not synchronized across time zones; market prices can differ from NAV; Yahoo is a secondary price source; adjusted-price methodology is vendor-controlled; capture ratios are extremely sensitive to the selected months; and 2026's extraordinary semiconductor moves make annualised conditional figures unusually large. These figures describe path behaviour, not expected future returns. Raw endpoint pattern: [Yahoo chart API—SOXX](https://query2.finance.yahoo.com/v8/finance/chart/SOXX?period1=1622505600&period2=1787616000&interval=1mo&events=div%2Csplits). The USD listing and inception details for SEMI are confirmed by [iShares](https://www.ishares.com/uk/professionals/en/products/319084/ishares-msci-global-semiconductors-ucits-etf?switchLocale=y).

### Decision implications from the combined evidence

- **Best broad UCITS core:** iShares Global Semis UCITS has by far the broadest portfolio and direct Asian listings, but its 57x P/E and 11x P/B show that breadth does not currently mean cheapness. Its recent capture was the most aggressive of the compared USD lines.
- **Best concentrated UCITS expression:** VanEck UCITS has lower reported P/E and better recent capture asymmetry, but nearly four-fifths in ten names. It is economically closer to a focused leaders portfolio than a global industry basket.
- **Best US leader/momentum expression:** SMH had the strongest 3y and 5y up/down capture ratio among US funds, alongside the clearest Nvidia concentration risk.
- **Best valuation/breadth counterweight:** XSD had the lowest P/B and a substantially lower forward P/E, plus less than 30% in its top ten. The price was materially worse downside capture—equal weighting exposes the portfolio to weaker, smaller cyclical companies as well as future winners.
- **Best fee:** SOXQ at 0.19% closely resembles the SOXX/PHLX large-cap exposure. The capture histories were also very close, so fee and live spread favour SOXQ if a US-domiciled fund is otherwise acceptable.
- **Asia satellite, not core substitute:** 3119 provides the clearest Taiwan/Korea/Japan/China production-chain exposure but costs 0.68%, is highly top-three concentrated, and its unusual capture result should not be extrapolated.

## Follow-up: did Samsung and SK hynix fall on market dynamics rather than fundamentals?

### Bottom line

**Mostly yes, based on the public evidence through 25 August 2026—but not “purely” market dynamics.** Both Korean shares corrected much more sharply from June highs than broad semiconductor ETFs, even as each reported record Q2 results, improving memory pricing, supply constraints and stronger balance-sheet/cash evidence. The more defensible interpretation is **expectation and valuation compression after extraordinary gains**, mixed with genuine company-specific risks: Samsung still has to prove durable HBM leadership and foundry profitability; SK hynix has extreme memory/HBM/customer-cycle exposure, large capacity commitments and fresh-share dilution from its US ADR offering.

### Fundamental evidence: Samsung Electronics

- Q2 revenue was **KRW 171.5tn**, up 28% QoQ, and operating profit **KRW 89.5tn**, both quarterly records. Device Solutions generated KRW 127.5tn revenue and KRW 89.2tn operating profit; memory revenue and profit were records. Samsung cited industry-wide price increases, record server mix, greater HBM4 sales and first HBM4E sample shipments. It expects H2 server DRAM, enterprise SSD and HBM demand to accelerate and supply constraints to persist. [Samsung Q2 results](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-announces-second-quarter-2026-results)
- This is clear evidence **against near-term memory-fundamental deterioration**. It is also a major improvement from the earlier concern that Samsung was behind in HBM: HBM4 commercial shipments and HBM4E sampling show progress. They do not prove yields, customer qualification breadth or sustainable share equal to SK hynix.
- Foundry improved on HBM base-die and US orders, with second-generation 2nm mobile ramp and double-digit H2 revenue-growth target. But Samsung only said earnings improved materially *before incentive-related provisions* and did not disclose a standalone foundry profit. Continued advanced-node utilisation, yield, customer conversion and capital intensity remain material execution risks.
- Non-semiconductor businesses were not universally strong: DX revenue fell 9% QoQ; mobile/networks and visual-display/appliances recorded slight losses under component-cost pressure. Samsung is therefore a diversified electronics company, not a pure HBM instrument.
- Balance-sheet/FCF evidence is supportive: the board estimated **KRW 90–110tn** of 2026 shareholder returns, including about KRW 30tn dividends and a KRW 15tn employee-compensation buyback, under its policy to return 50% of three-year FCF. That scale would be difficult to reconcile with immediate financial distress, although an employee-compensation buyback is not economically identical to cancellation. [Samsung shareholder return](https://news.samsung.com/global/samsung-electronics-to-implement-largest-ever-shareholder-return-in-2026-estimated-at-krw-90-to-110-trillion)

**Samsung-specific reasons the discount may be rational:** HBM share/yield/customer qualification still has to be demonstrated over multiple quarters; foundry economics remain opaque and capital-heavy; consumer/mobile margins weakened; conglomerate complexity dilutes pure memory upside; and the stock had already risen 264% in a year before the correction endpoint.

### Fundamental evidence: SK hynix

- Q2 revenue reached **KRW 79.319tn** (+51% QoQ, +257% YoY), operating profit **KRW 60.543tn** (+61% QoQ, +557% YoY), and operating margin **76%**. DRAM and NAND prices rose significantly QoQ and high-value HBM, AI-server DRAM and enterprise SSD drove profitability. These are not deteriorating reported fundamentals. [SK hynix Q2 results](https://news.skhynix.com/en/q2-2026-business-results/)
- HBM4 began mass shipments in Q2 and is scheduled to ramp in H2; HBM4E samples were completed in H1. SK hynix said HBM4 met customer speed requirements with leading power efficiency and cost competitiveness. It had long-term agreements with around ten customers and was discussing more, supporting demand visibility.
- Financial health improved sharply: cash and equivalents were **KRW 88tn**, debt fell to KRW 18.6tn, and net cash reached **KRW 69.4tn**. This argues strongly against a balance-sheet-driven selloff.
- The counterweight is capex/cycle risk. SK hynix is accelerating M15X production, preparing Yongin Phase 1 for early 2027, and planning P&T7 packaging, M17 NAND and a new cluster. Management promises phased investment and capex discipline, but at 76% operating margins the risk is that current prices/margins induce industry supply and later mean reversion.
- Customer concentration has two faces: approximately ten LTAs improve visibility, but leading-edge HBM demand still depends on a small number of AI accelerator/platform customers and hyperscalers. Qualification delay, a customer architecture shift, or AI-capex moderation can move earnings expectations abruptly.
- A concrete non-operating supply event occurred in July: the Nasdaq ADR listing involved up to **17.79m new common shares, about 2.5% of pre-offering shares**, with each ADS representing one-tenth of a common share. The offering broadened access and funded growth but diluted existing holders and created price-discovery/flow pressure around the local and ADR lines. [SEC registration](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2120882/000119312526280172/d32785df1.htm) [SEC ADS ratio](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2120882/000119312526299071/d28812d8a12b.htm)

**SK hynix-specific reasons the discount may be rational:** a 549% one-year share-price rise, very high peak-cycle margins, HBM/customer concentration, a huge multi-year capacity program, 2.5% new-share issuance, and the possibility that results can be excellent yet below expectations already embedded in the price.

### Thesis verdict

There is no primary-source evidence of an operating collapse through Q2. On the contrary, revenue, profit, memory pricing, HBM shipments and cash positions improved. Therefore **the excess drawdowns are more consistent with de-risking, profit-taking, expectation resets, dilution/flow effects and the higher cyclicality of single names** than with deterioration in contemporaneous fundamentals. That conclusion is an inference, not something the companies can verify.

The thesis fails if subsequent evidence shows any of the following: HBM4/HBM4E qualification or yield problems; large AI customers reducing orders; DRAM/NAND contract-price reversal or inventory build; Samsung foundry losses widening despite high utilisation; SK hynix capex/free-cash-flow turning materially worse; or conventional memory demand weakening enough to offset AI products.

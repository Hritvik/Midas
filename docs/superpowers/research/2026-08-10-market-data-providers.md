# Market Data Providers For Initial Stocks/ETF Daily History

Date: 2026-08-10

Scope: initial Midas investment support for daily historical prices for listed stocks and ETFs, with particular attention to U.S./international markets and Indian NSE/BSE coverage. This note does not select a provider.

## Required Slice

Midas needs legal API access to daily OHLCV history, stable symbol lookup, explicit exchange/currency metadata, adjusted or reconstructable total-return-ish series, and enough retention rights to persist fetched prices in the user's local ledger-derived projections. A provider that supports raw OHLC plus split/dividend events is preferable to one that exposes only adjusted close, because Midas can keep valuation math auditable and avoid silent restatements.

## Verified Constraints

- Redistribution is the recurring legal boundary. Tiingo, Twelve Data, EODHD, Alpha Vantage, NSE, and Nasdaq all distinguish internal/personal use from commercial redistribution or third-party display. [Tiingo terms](https://app.tiingo.com/tos/), [Twelve Data support](https://support.twelvedata.com/en/articles/5332349-commercial-and-personal-usage), [EODHD terms](https://eodhd.com/financial-apis/terms-conditions), [Alpha Vantage terms](https://www.alphavantage.co/terms_of_service/), [NSE data policy](https://www.nseindia.com/static/market-data/nse-data-policy), [Nasdaq policy notice](https://www.nasdaqtrader.com/TraderNews.aspx?id=DN2024-5)
- Retention is not a generic right. EODHD explicitly allows private storage during an active subscription but requires deletion within one month after subscription termination. [EODHD terms](https://eodhd.com/financial-apis/terms-conditions)
- Adjusted-series semantics differ materially. Tiingo provides adjusted OHLCV and split/dividend fields; EODHD provides raw OHLC plus split/dividend-adjusted close; Alpha Vantage daily adjusted exposes raw OHLCV, adjusted close, splits, and dividends; Massive provides split-adjusted historical data by default but not dividend-adjusted data. [Tiingo docs](https://www.tiingo.com/documentation/end-of-day), [EODHD docs](https://eodhd.com/financial-apis/api-for-historical-data-and-volumes), [Alpha Vantage docs](https://www.alphavantage.co/documentation/), [Massive FAQ](https://massive.com/knowledge-base/article/is-massives-stock-data-adjusted-for-splits-or-dividends)
- Indian coverage is the main practical divide. Twelve Data and EODHD provide global/India-capable coverage on paper; Tiingo and Massive are not verified for India in the reviewed official pages; NSE direct and Indian vendors are local-authoritative but contract-heavy. [Twelve Data stocks](https://twelvedata.com/stocks), [EODHD docs](https://eodhd.com/financial-apis/api-for-historical-data-and-volumes), [Tiingo EOD product](https://www.tiingo.com/products/end-of-day-stock-price-data), [Massive stocks](https://massive.com/stocks), [NSE EOD/historical](https://www.nseindia.com/static/market-data/eod-historical-data-subscription)
- Trading calendars are not uniformly solved. Massive exposes current market status and upcoming market holidays, but says upcoming holidays are forward-looking only; other reviewed global APIs did not yield a verified full historical exchange-calendar product in their official pages. [Massive REST overview](https://massive.com/docs/rest/stocks/overview)

## Inferences For Midas

- For a single-user, non-commercial Phase 0/1 prototype, providers with explicit internal-use allowances can support local valuation features, but Midas should keep provider data out of any shared public UI until commercial/display/redistribution rights are contracted.
- Midas should store provider provenance per price row: provider, symbol, exchange, currency, as-of date, raw/adjusted semantics, fetched-at timestamp, and subscription/license class. This is needed because adjusted definitions and retention rights differ by provider.
- If Indian holdings are in the initial slice, a U.S.-specialist provider alone is insufficient. Either choose a global provider with verified India plan access or pair a U.S. provider with an Indian authorized vendor/direct NSE source.
- Do not assume adjusted close is enough for all valuation and return use cases. A reconstructable model using raw OHLC, split factors, dividends, and explicit adjustment policy is easier to audit.
- A provider contract check is a build prerequisite if Midas will persist fetched data long-term, ship to other users, or expose prices in a hosted UI.

## Open Questions Before Selection

1. Is Midas initially personal-only/local, or will it be hosted for any other user? This changes whether personal/internal plans are enough.
2. Must historical prices remain usable after a subscription is cancelled? If yes, EODHD's deletion clause is a blocker unless separately contracted.
3. Are Indian ETFs/stocks required in the first implementation, or can Phase 1 launch with U.S./international ex-India and add India through a licensed local source later?
4. Does Midas need dividend-inclusive performance, or only market-value marks? Dividend-inclusive performance requires either adjusted OHLCV or raw dividends plus a documented adjustment engine.
5. What is the acceptable symbol-resolution strategy: provider-native symbols only, or an internal instrument master keyed by ISIN/exchange/MIC with provider aliases?
